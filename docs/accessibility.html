Accessibility

We want this site to work for everybody. Here is what we tested, and honestly, what we did not.

How we are doing

This site aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.1 at level AA. We believe it does, but we have not had it audited by anyone outside the company, so treat that as our own assessment rather than a certificate.

What we checked, and fixed

  • Every colour pair on the page, in both the light and dark themes, against the AA contrast thresholds. Three shades were too pale on the grey bands and were darkened.
  • Every image carries a written description, or an empty one where the image is decoration and should be skipped rather than announced.
  • One main heading per page, with the rest in order and no levels skipped. Two footer headings were at the wrong level and were corrected.
  • A skip link as the first thing you reach with the keyboard, so you can jump the menu.
  • A visible focus outline on everything you can reach with a keyboard. It is never removed.
  • Links and buttons are at least 44 pixels tall, so they can be hit without precision.
  • Pinch zoom is not blocked, text reflows down to a narrow screen, and the page does not scroll sideways at any width.
  • Movement respects the reduce motion setting in your operating system.
  • The page is written in plain HTML, so it works with JavaScript turned off.

What we have not checked

This matters more than the list above, because an automated check finds somewhere around half of real accessibility problems. The rest needs a person.

  • We have not tested this site with a screen reader, with voice control, or with a switch device. If you use one and something reads badly, we would genuinely like to know.
  • Whether our image descriptions are useful is a judgement we made ourselves. The screenshots are dense, and a written description of one is never the whole picture.
  • We have not tested with magnification software beyond browser zoom.

Known limitations

  • The screenshots show a lot of information at once. Each has a description of what it demonstrates rather than a reading of every element in it, because the latter would be unusable. The text beside each screenshot explains the same feature, so nothing is only available in an image.
  • The Nextup software itself is a separate thing from this site and has been built to the same rules, but it has had no independent accessibility audit either.

Telling us about a barrier

Two routes, because the one you need might be the one that is broken.

  • Email hello@lightmorphic.co.uk.
  • Open an issue on the project's issue tracker.

Or write to Lightmorphic Ltd, 82A James Carter Road, Mildenhall, IP28 7DE. We reply within five working days.

If you are not happy with our answer, you can contact the Equality Advisory and Support Service, who advise on rights under the Equality Act 2010.
